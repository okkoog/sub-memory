## Quick Start & Opening

The required prerequisite is "Go Fishing"; this project is built against the Go Fishing 2.4.0 material content. The cuisine expansion installs independently — the fishing mod itself can be installed without the cuisine expansion. There is no need to treat old fish packs, ItemForge, or old standalone English patches as prerequisites. Chinese, English, and Russian switch with the game language.

Contact Willie to open a cuisine business, pay 1000 credits, and confirm receipt of the opening kit. The kit has 11 items: a cuisine operation certificate, the Chef Association cuisine manual, a cooking station, a stockpot, a frying pan, a grill, plus one bottle each of white vinegar, soy sauce, dark soy sauce, cooking wine, and a sugar jar. If your inventory is full or items temporarily cannot spawn, unredeemed gifts are kept pending; contact Willie again after clearing space, and do not re-open the business just to re-claim items.

For your first dish, use small yellow croaker to make "A Clear Broth": put the fish in the ingredient area, install the stockpot, set high heat, ensure the battery has at least 5 available energy, keep the output area clear, then click craft. Recipes without required seasonings need no extra oil. To practice sashimi, prepare large yellow croaker meat, soy sauce, and white vinegar, clear the cookware area, and set no heat.

After crafting, read the dish description to check the main ingredient, method, seasoning, portion, score, and ingredient value. A non-preferred main ingredient can still discover the matching recipe, but the result becomes a fish bento box. To receive orders, check "contact cuisine diners" in night service and process in-store orders the next day.

## Cooking Station & Consumption

The ingredient/seasoning area holds the main ingredient and food seasonings; the input area has been expanded to 12×5 and can hold a 12×2 adult swordfish sideways. Seasonings go in the seasoning area; only one pot or grill can be installed in the cookware area. The output area receives finished dishes, the battery area provides energy, and the manual area is for reference; the idle cookware area stores spare cookware. To switch methods, remove the old cookware first — you cannot install multiple pieces at once.

| Method | Cookware | Heat | Fish Oil Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blanch (白灼) | Stockpot | High | No fish oil |
| Stew (炖煮) | Stockpot | Low | No fish oil |
| Steam (清蒸) | Stockpot | Medium | No fish oil |
| Pan-fry (煎制) | Frying pan | Medium | No fish oil |
| Deep-fry (油炸) | Frying pan | High | Must add fish oil |
| Char-grill (碳烤) | Grill | Medium or high | Per recipe |
| Sashimi (鱼生) | Cookware area empty | No heat | No fish oil |

The heat button cycles no heat → low → medium → high. Sashimi consumes no power; every other craft consumes 5 energy. If energy is insufficient, the method is invalid, or the output area cannot fit the result, crafting will not submit normally; resolve the indicated problem first.

White vinegar, soy sauce, dark soy sauce, cooking wine, and sugar are the five bottled seasonings of this expansion, each with 20 uses — one use per craft, removed only when depleted. Salt, super salt, chili, blazing chili, and fish oil belong to the fishing-side seasonings and do not follow this 20-use rule: they are consumed entirely when added. Note especially that fish oil is a seasoning and goes in the seasoning area, not the ordinary food seasoning area.

Actual dishes produced (including fish bento boxes and unpalatable meals) consume ingredients and the corresponding seasoning uses. Crafting consumes ALL ingredients in the input area, not just one fish from a stack; ordinary seasonings and fishing-side seasonings are also counted by the amount added. Split apart stacks you do not intend to use before crafting. Cookware is not consumed as an ingredient. Main ingredients that allow multiple pieces let you combine multiple items of the same identity and drying state; some rare main ingredients allow only a single piece. Different identities, or mixing fresh fish with dried fish, will fail the crafting condition.

Saltiness and spiciness each have three tiers: no corresponding seasoning = light salt / no chili; 1 item = normal salt / normal chili; 2 or more = heavy salt / extra chili. Ordinary salt and super salt cannot be mixed, nor can ordinary chili and blazing chili, or the recipe will not match. To raise a tier, increase the count of the same item. A single craft uses at most three different seasonings; dishes with three required seasonings need attention to whether there is room to satisfy order tastes.

## Ingredients & Compatibility

A whole fish must be a recognizable native whole-fish item; roe and fry do not count as whole-fish main ingredients. The growth stage of a fish item does not need to be adult yet. Dead fish does not automatically equal dried fish: dried fish requires the fish to be dead AND have the fishing system's air-dried state. Amber Grilled Dried Fish uses air-dried volcanic fish; fresh volcanic fish cannot substitute for that category.

Fish meat and parts are identified by source species; same-named materials are not necessarily equivalent. Materials produced by the current fishing system carry a source record that cooking reads; it will not invent a source or calories to let a material pass. Some old dedicated materials keep compatibility recognition, but do not expect same-named meat from other mods to work.

Small and large finished canned fish are identified by canning batch: salt-cured corresponds to salted fish meat, spicy to spicy fish meat, oil-marinated to oil-marinated fish meat. Plain cans are processed fish meat, but currently have no dedicated recipe and cannot be used for sashimi. Processed-meat recipes require matching identity — you cannot swap in another can and expect a bento-box version.

A can must be a single item with a valid batch, size, processing method, and remaining calories; calories must be greater than zero and no higher than the initial batch calories. Empty cans, damaged records, or items without a valid batch do not count as usable main ingredients. A partially-eaten can with valid remaining calories does not magically restore its initial calories.

Raw meat sourced from Emperor red-armor crab is recognized under the crab cuisine identity; ordinary raw meat cannot pass. Shark meat and shark tail steaks are recognized under the fish-meat category; shark head is used for "Great Rift".

## Dish Scoring & Revenue

Hitting a recipe with the preferred main ingredient yields the named dish; hitting the recipe with a non-preferred main ingredient of the same type yields a "fish bento box", whose description retains the matching recipe. Not hitting any recipe yields an "unpalatable meal". A bento box is not a failure: it still discovers the recipe and carries recipe identity for order fulfillment; an unpalatable meal has no valid recipe identity and its quality is fixed very low.

A matched recipe's score = recipe target − main-ingredient penalty + that main ingredient's suitability modifier for the method + portion modifier, finally clamped to 1–100. The preferred main ingredient incurs no penalty; substitute raw meat deducts 14, other substitute main ingredients deduct 6 to 40 based on the suitability gap from the preferred. Mouse fish with chili adds an extra 5 points. Tender meat, heavy whole fish, and drying differences all affect suitability — not every expensive fish scores higher.

Portion considers only the total weight of all main ingredients, not seasoning weight: positive weight up to 150 g is light; 151–500 g is standard; 501–1500 g is large; over 1500 g is a feast. When no positive weight is obtained, standard portion applies. The four tiers modify the score by −2, 0, +2, +3 respectively. Multiple same-identity main ingredients in one pot add total weight and input cost, but still produce a single dish.

Finished ingredient value includes the main ingredients, seasonings, and seasonings used this craft. The five bottled seasonings are counted by (single-bottle value ÷ 20, rounded down, at least 1) per use; other materials are counted by (per-item value, at least 1) × number added. A matched recipe's price = ingredient value × (1 + score ÷ 100), rounded (half up) and at least 1. When unmatched, score is fixed at 15 and price is half the ingredient value, rounded and at least 1.

Calories sum the actual calories of all added ingredients; bottled seasonings contribute their non-negative calories ÷ 20 (rounded down) per use, others accumulate by count. Fresh whole fish reads edible meat calories; dead fish prioritizes corpse calories; dried fish, processed materials, and cans read current calories. Thus weight, score, calories, and price are different metrics — large portion does not guarantee high calories, and high price does not automatically guarantee high rating.

### Example

Suppose a dish's ingredient value is 100 and final score is 90; its list price is 100×1.9 = 190. Delivered to an ordinary diner with 5 stars, the reward is 100×1.9×1.0×1.1 = 209; the same dish with 3 stars is 100×1.9×1.0×0.85, settled by final rounding. Here 90 is the dish score, while 5-star or 3-star is this order's rating.

Note: final rounding for dish price and order reward follows existing calculation; exact halves round to the nearest even number (e.g., 160.5 → 160, 161.5 → 162).

## Reservations & Order Fulfillment

After opening, check "contact cuisine diners" in night service — free each night, diners arrive the next day; you need one cooking station you own. Unchecking only stops future reservations; existing unfinished orders persist across nights and will not re-reserve until completed. The current flow no longer requires holding or showing the certificate, nor relies on random daytime diner draws.

Orders are drawn from discovered recipes; with no available record it falls back to beginner recipes. At average rating 4 stars, two-dish probability is 20%; at 5 stars, 40%; below 4 stars no two-dish draw, and at least two selectable recipes are required. Two-dish orders require two different recipes sharing taste conditions, delivered and paid separately; the rating is recorded only after both dishes are completed.

| Diner Tier | Minimum Quality Score | Reward Tier Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary diner | 50 | 1.0 |
| Discerning diner | 60 | 1.1 |
| Veteran diner | 70 | 1.2 |
| Uptown diner | 80 | 1.3 |

Higher reputation raises the chance of drawing high-tier diners — it is not that only uptown diners come after high stars. Read the order's saltiness, spiciness, quality, large-portion, or valuable-ingredient requirements. Large-portion requires at least large; the valuable condition checks ingredient value of at least 150, not the finished price. Two-dish orders select two recipes whose tastes can both be satisfied; complete and deliver one at a time.

Deliver dishes crafted in the current version with complete cuisine records to order diners. A fish bento box with the correct recipe can be delivered and rated by its actual quality; old-version dishes do not count for this order. A single-dish order with the wrong recipe may still be accepted and penalized in rating; an unpalatable meal is forced to 1 star — do not treat the deliver button as a lossless taste test. Two-dish orders reject dishes not among the two recipes or already delivered, indicating which dish is still missing.

Rating starts from 5 stars: wrong dish −2, wrong method −2; failing each of saltiness, spiciness, portion, and ingredient value −1; insufficient quality −1, or −2 if 10 points or more below the threshold; finally clamped to 1–5 stars. A two-dish order's stars are the average of the two, rounded up on half. The last 10 order ratings form the reputation record, viewable on the certificate.

Each reward = ingredient value × (1 + score ÷ 100) × diner tier multiplier × star multiplier, finally rounded and at least 1. The star multipliers for 1–5 stars are 0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 1.0, 1.1. Reward is recalculated with the unrounded formula — you cannot take the finished price and multiply again; two-dish orders settle each dish separately.

## Certificate & Old Saves

The certificate now displays shop reputation and no longer gates cuisine qualification or diner recruitment. Recipe discovery, preferred-main-ingredient confirmation, the last 10 ratings, and craft preview are saved with the shop save; moving or losing the certificate should not reset this progress. Discovering a recipe and confirming the preferred main ingredient are two things: make a bento box first to discover the recipe, then use the preferred material to confirm the best pairing.

Old certificates and old cooking stations' historical records have a one-time migration; old orders also have compatibility migration. Keep old items and load the save normally, then check the manual and certificate to confirm recovery — do not try to game progress by repeatedly rebuilding certificates. Old dishes are bound by the current delivery rules; recraft to take new orders.

## FAQ

- **All materials present but can't craft?** Check whether you own them, whether main-ingredient identity is consistent, whether only one cookware is installed, whether heat is correct, whether fish oil is misplaced, whether sashimi still has cookware, and whether hot dishes have at least 5 energy.
- **Why did I get an unpalatable meal?** Compare against the full recipe; check main-ingredient category, missing materials, extra materials, and mixing two salts or two chilis. Adding soy sauce or fish oil not required by the recipe may also invalidate it.
- **Why is it a fish bento box?** Method and seasonings match, but the main ingredient is not the dish's preferred; read the matching recipe in the description, then use the correct species and material form.
- **Why did a whole stack disappear?** Input consumes the entire batch this craft; split first. Only the five new bottled seasonings follow the 20-use rule.
- **Why no diners today?** Check whether you checked "contact" last night, whether you've opened and own a cooking station, and whether there are unfinished reservations; checking during the day does not immediately generate today's new order.
- **Output area full?** Remove the finished dish first, then craft; output that cannot fit consumes no materials, so no need to rebuy ingredients.
